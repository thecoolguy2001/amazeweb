Nothing Comes
From Nothing

Caledonian Energy · 2026 · 90s broadcast · nine shoot days

A 90-second film about where power actually comes from, shot across four sites in weather that was never once forecast correctly.

Brief usMore work

The brief was a sentence and it was the right sentence.

“People think electricity comes out of the wall.” That was it. The client had spent two years on a campaign about tariffs and had worked out that nobody cared, because nobody could picture the thing being sold.

So we filmed the whole chain, backwards: a kettle in a flat in Govan, the substation three streets away, the line, the converter station, the turbine, the sea. Ninety seconds, one continuous voice, no music until the last eleven.

Nine shoot days in February and March. Four of them were lost to weather, which we had budgeted for, and one was lost to a gate nobody had the key to, which we had not. The sea unit worked from a crew transfer vessel out of Nigg with a two-hour window either side of slack water.

The grade is deliberately cold until the kettle at the end. Sound was the hard part and the part we are proudest of: the turbine interior was recorded clean and is in the final mix almost untouched.
