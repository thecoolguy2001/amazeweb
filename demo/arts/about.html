A former tile works, twenty minutes from the water.

Ines Varela was born in Setúbal in 1984 and has worked there, in the same building, since 2013.

The paintings begin outdoors, in gouache, usually in one sitting. They are finished in the studio over months, in oil and distemper, and the colour almost always drifts away from the place itself — darker, flatter, more like the memory of weather than a record of it.

The ground she keeps returning to is reclaimed marshland: land that is only land because a pump is running somewhere. Pumping stations, sluices, reed beds, the hour before rain. She has painted the same four kilometres of it for thirteen years.

She studied painting at the Faculdade de Belas-Artes in Lisbon and spent two years as a technician in a ceramics factory, which is where the ash in the Kiln Studies comes from. The kiln in the studio still works; it is used about twice a year.

Studio visits happen on the first Thursday of each month, by appointment, two at a time. Curators, writers and collectors are all welcome; so is anyone who has walked out from the station.
