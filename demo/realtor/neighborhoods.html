Four neighborhoods, and what is actually true about them.

Written from walking the streets and from seventeen years of sales, not from the assessor's database. Where something is a problem, it says so.

Mill Quarter

Brick terraces behind the old flour mill, almost all of them 1900 to 1925, almost all three bedrooms. Flat, walkable, and the part of the east side that has changed least.

Watch for: no off-street parking anywhere above Calder, and the permit list runs about four months. The three streets below Calder get the Saturday market traffic from seven in the morning.

Ferry Point

The newest part of the east side and the only one with lifts and parking in the same building. Two blocks from 2006, one from 2014, and a row of converted warehouses along the water.

Watch for: everything below Water Lane is in the flood zone. Insurance is available but it is not cheap, and two buildings have had claims. Ask us for the map before you offer.

Alder Hill

The expensive one. Detached and semi-detached houses from the 1930s to the 1960s on wide streets, most with gardens backing onto the park. Where people move when the terrace gets too small.

Watch for: the 1968 estate at the top was built with a flat roof system that has needed replacing on most of the houses. If it has not been done, budget $40,000 and read the survey carefully.

Median price$1.02m
Price per sq ft$388
Walk score54

Eastbank

Townhouses from the late nineties around the old wharf, plus the shops and the office. Garages, small gardens, and the best bus route on this side of the river.

Watch for: the service charge on the wharf development covers the river wall and went up 18% in 2025. It is justified, but read what it is for.
